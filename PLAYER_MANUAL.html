How to Play

Win by being the last player with at least one country. You start with one random country and 10 troops.

Find Your Way Around

Drag the globe to look around and scroll over it to zoom. Select a country or its label to see its details and available actions in the right toolbar. If the toolbar is closed, select Expand toolbar. Choose Reset at the bottom right to return to your strongest country. Open Log at the bottom of the map to review events and dice rolls you were able to see.

You can inspect the map between turns. On an unowned country, Mag stands for magnitude, the country value used for claims, recruits, and nuclear retaliation.

Regions and Networks

A region is a group of territories on the board. For example, Japan and South Korea are both in East Asia. Owning any territory in a region lets you see the names of the others. Owning every territory in the region earns bonus recruits at the start of each round. You can also attack within a region without a land or sea border, using one attack die. Sharing a region by itself does not let you transfer troops.

A network links separate territories, often parts of the same real-world country. For example, Alaska and the Hawaiian Islands are in the United States network even though the game places them in different regions. Owning one gives you full information about the others. If you own both ends of the link, you can transfer troops directly between them. To attack across a network link, you still need a land or sea border or a shared region.

Select a territory to see its Region and, when you have full information, its Network. Check Region Progress in the toolbar for the recruit bonus and how many territories you still need.

What You Can See

Each player has their own view of the board. Select a country and look for the visibility label in the toolbar.

  • Full information. For countries marked Owned, Border or network, or Global, you can see the name, region, network (if any), magnitude, owner or unowned status, troop count, and whether it is a capital. Your own countries, their land and sea neighbors, and countries directly linked to yours by a network all give you full information.
  • Partial information. A country marked Same region shares a region with one you own, but you have no closer connection to it. You see only its name and region. Its owner, troops, magnitude, network, and symbols stay hidden. It could belong to an opponent or be unowned.
  • Hidden. Countries outside those connections and regions are left off your map. They can still be in play.

Claim or conquer new countries to extend your view. Each one reveals its neighbors and network connections in full, and gives you partial information on any newly reached region. For full information worldwide, hold any one of the United States, China, Russia, India, or Japan, or hold Germany, Italy, and Czechia together. Your view can shrink when you lose a country that gave you visibility.

Take Your Turn

  1. Claim or attack. Select one of your countries with at least two troops. Use the available actions in the toolbar. You can make multiple claims and attacks, including from newly acquired countries.
  2. Move troops. Select Finished Attacking when you are ready. Select one of your countries, pick a destination under Transfer to, enter the number of troops, and choose Transfer. Transfers can travel through your own countries connected by land, sea, or a shared network. Being in the same region alone is insufficient. The amount starts at the most you can move.
  3. Hand over the turn. Select End Turn, even if you made no transfers. You must leave at least one troop behind when attacking, claiming, or transferring from a country.

The turn order goes down the player list and then back up. With three players it runs A, B, C, C, B, A, then repeats. A new round can begin after any turn, with a 1-in-N chance where N is the number of active players. A new round also begins if all active players pass in succession. The next player in the turn order still goes next.

Claim an Unowned Country

Select one of your countries with a land or sea connection to the unowned country. Pick the target under Claim, enter at least its magnitude in troops, and select Claim. Even a country with magnitude zero needs one troop. Those troops move into the new country.

Attack Another Country

Pick a target under Attack, choose the number of attack dice and how many troops to move if you win, then select Attack. Land attacks allow up to three dice, sea attacks up to two, and same-region attacks without a border up to one. You need one movable troop per die. The defender rolls up to two dice, limited by their troops.

Compare the highest die on each side, then the next highest if both sides rolled enough dice. The lower roll loses one troop and the defender wins ties. If the defender loses their last troop, you take the country and move in troops while keeping one behind. When you attack a country you can only partially see, the result may disclose just an attacking loss, a successful conquest or claim, or no visible outcome.

Attack to End State repeats the attack with the maximum dice and moves as many troops as possible if you win. It stops when the target is taken, the attack can no longer continue, or a retaliation decision is needed.

Place Recruits

When the game asks you to place recruits, select one of your countries, enter an amount, and choose Place Recruits. Repeat until none are left. Once everyone has finished placing, play resumes.

At the start of a new round, add up the magnitude of your countries and divide by 25. You receive whole recruits, and any fraction carries into the next round. Owning every country in a region earns bonus recruits. Check Region Progress in the toolbar to see how many countries remain and the bonus for completing each region. If you started with a nuclear country, your first region bonus recruits are withheld up to that country's starting magnitude.

Read the Map Symbols

  • Capital. Your longest-held country is your capital. If you lose it, your next longest-held country takes over. A capital rolls one extra die in attack or defense and discards its lowest roll before comparison. It still needs at least two troops to attack.
  • Satellite. This marks a country that can help you unlock worldwide visibility. If you own two of Germany, Italy, and Czechia and can see the third is unowned, the symbol marks the remaining country.
  • Nuclear. Attacking or claiming this country can trigger retaliation.
  • Missile. This nuclear country has long-range missiles (ICBMs) and can retaliate against visible opponents' countries beyond its usual attack range.

Look for these symbols under country labels. A country with partial information shows no symbols, even if it has one of these abilities. When an ability depends on who owns another country, you also need enough information to confirm that ownership.

China, France, India, Israel, North Korea, Pakistan, Russia, the United Kingdom, and the United States are nuclear. All of those except Pakistan have ICBMs. Belarus becomes nuclear when its owner also holds Russia. Belgium, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Turkey become nuclear when their owner also holds the United States.

Respond to Nuclear Retaliation

An unowned nuclear country retaliates automatically. If your nuclear country is attacked, play pauses for your decision. Select a target in the toolbar and choose Retaliate, or choose Do Not Retaliate to pass or stop. Each selection removes troops up to the remaining limit, which starts at the retaliating country's magnitude. A country that loses all its troops becomes unowned.

Most nuclear countries can retaliate against land or sea neighbors and countries in the same region. The toolbar lists the available targets. Hitting another player's nuclear country can give them a chance to retaliate in turn. Losing your last country does not take away a retaliation decision you are owed.

Shared Staging Areas

Antarctica and Bir Tawil can hold troops from different players. They cannot be claimed or attacked. Access to Bir Tawil requires Egypt or The Sudan. Access to Antarctica requires Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Chile, China, Czechia, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Russia, South Africa, South Korea, Ukraine, the United Kingdom, the United States, or Uruguay.

Finish the Game

You are eliminated when you lose your last country. Troops in shared staging areas do not keep you in play. The game ends when only one player remains. You can also choose Forfeit in the toolbar to leave the game and give up your countries.
